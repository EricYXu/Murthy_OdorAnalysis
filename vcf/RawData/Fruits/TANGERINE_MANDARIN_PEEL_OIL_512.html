Additional info

Origin: Some confusion exists as to the proper use of the word tangerine. Tangerines were originally a group of Moroccan mandarin varieties (and their later hybrids) exported through the port of Tangier (Tangerin French), a town at the northern tip of Morocco. More recently the name tangerine has been used of the other parent of mandarin × orange and mandarin × grapefruit hybrids in order to secure the names tangor andtangelo. As Dancywas one of the first varieties used in these hybrids From Citrus pages
Use: The oil is used in sweets, gelatins, ice-cream, chewing gum, pastry and confectioneries. It is also used in soft drinks, mixers, essences and flavourings as well as in mandarin liqueurs and other alcohol products.
Production: Mandarin oils are obtained by cold pressing followed by separation in a water and oil fraction and by distillation. Both types of oil are compiled together.
Data (not) used: Almost all literature gives the volatile composition as percentage of the essential oil. Changes in volatile composition due to ripening and storage are not taken into consideration.
