Additional info

Origin: Mediterranean mandarin(Willowleafin the US) is the common mandarin of the Mediterranean basin, which is known by many names including Ba Ahmed (Morocco), Blida, Boufarik and Bougie (Algeria), Bodrum (Turkey), Paterno and Palermo (Italy), Nice and Provence (France), Valencia (Spain), and Setúbal (Portugal). In comparison with the other mandarins, the most distinctive characteristics of the Mediterranean mandarin include: (1) the small size and narrow-lanceolate form of the leaves and the special nature and aroma of the oil they contain; (2) the mild and pleasantly aromatic flavour of the juice; (3) the distinctive nature and fragrance of the rind oil; and (4) the plump and almost spherical seeds. From citruspages
Use: The rind oil is used in the preparation of perfumes and toilet waters and for the flavouring of confections and carbonated beverages. Petitgrain oil, which is distilled from the prunings and comes mainly from the leaves, has a strong and characteristic odour.
Production: Citrus oils are often obtained by cold pressing or distillation of the peel, but also of the whole fruit.
Data (not) used: Almost all literature gives the volatile composition as percentage of the essential oil. Changes in volatile composition due to ripening and storage are not taken into consideration
