Additional info

Origin: Mandarins (Citrus reticulata) were grown in China and Japan on a large scale since the 16th century. Mandarin was the last of the important citrus fruits to arrive in Europe as late as the early 19th century. Commercial production of mandarins started in the early 1900's. The biggest mandarin producers today are China, Spain, Japan, Brazil and Turkey. The biggest mandarin exporters are Spain, China, Morocco, Turkey and South Africa. Mandarin, its hybrids and relatives together form the largest group in the genus Citrus. The number of commercially important mandarin varieties almost equals the number of all other horticultural Citrus types combined. From Citrus pages
The classification of mandarins is rather complicated. We have classified clementines under Mandarin (Citrus reticulata), but tangerines considered as seperate product. Use: The oil is used in sweets, gelatins, ice-cream, chewing gum, pastry and confectioneries. It is also used in soft drinks, mixers, essences and flavourings as well as in mandarin liqueurs and other alcohol products.
Production: Mandarin oils are obtained by cold pressing followed by separation in a water and oil fraction and by distillation. Both types of oil are compiled together.
Data (not) used: Almost all literature gives the volatile composition as percentage of the essential oil. Changes in volatile composition due to ripening and storage are not taken into consideration.
