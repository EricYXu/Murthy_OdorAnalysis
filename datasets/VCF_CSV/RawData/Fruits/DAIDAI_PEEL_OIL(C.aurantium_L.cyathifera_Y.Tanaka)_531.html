Additional info

Origin: Daidai (Citrus aurantium L. var. cyathifera Tanaka) is a variety of the sour orange (C. aurantium L.) produced mainly in Japan and some parts of China. The daidai originated in the Himalayas. It spread to the Yangtze valley region and later to Japan. The name daidai, originally meaning ‘several generations’, originates from the fruit staying on the tree for several years if not picked. From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Use: The sour orange has been used for salad dressings, culinary, seasonings, and flavouring of beverages such as tea.
Production: Citrus oils are often obtained by cold pressing of the peel. These oils are more stable on storage than essence oils (obtained by extraction or distillation) because of the presence of non-volatile constituents which have antioxidative properties.
Data (not) used: Almost all literature gives the volatile composition as percentage of the essential oil. Changes in volatile composition due to storage are not taken into consideration
