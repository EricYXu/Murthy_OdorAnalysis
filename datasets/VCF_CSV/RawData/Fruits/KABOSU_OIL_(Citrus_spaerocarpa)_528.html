Additional info

Origin: Kabosu (Citrus sphaerocarpa Hort. ex Tanaka) is a sour citrus fruit produced in southern Japan in Oita Prefecture on Kyushu island. The Kabosu was brought over from China. It grows on a flowering plant with sharp thorns, and the fruit is harvested when still green but ripens to yellow. From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Use: The fruits are green and juicy and are closely related to the yuzu with the sharpness of lemon. In some Japanese dishes it is used instead of vinegar. It is used in salad dressings and seasonings, especially in grilled seafood and hotpots. The flavour is applied to liquor, jelly and cosmetics.
Production: Citrus oils are often obtained by cold pressing of the peel. These oils are more stable on storage than essence oils (obtained by extraction or distillation) because of the presence of non-volatile constituents which have antioxidative properties.
Data (not) used: Almost all literature gives the volatile composition as percentage of the essential oil. Changes in volatile composition due to ripening and storage are not taken into consideration
